    items:
      - name: "Test 1: A trial timeline that says who bills you"
        hypothesis: "If the paywall and Day 6 show the charge date, the amount, who bills you, and exactly where to cancel, fewer people will feel tricked, and just as many will still pay."
        control: "Today’s paywall."
        variant: "A timeline card, a “remind me 2 days before” option, and a direct path to the right cancel screen."
        primary: "Refund requests and billing tickets per 100 trial starts within 30 days of the first charge."
        guardrail: "Trial-to-paid rate."
      - name: "Test 2: Quick Sort right after the first sync"
        hypothesis: "Offering Quick Sort while motivation is highest will get more people to a clean month than waiting for a Day 1 nudge."
        control: "A Day 1 morning prompt."
        variant: "Quick Sort offered on the sync-complete screen, with “later” always available."
        primary: "Quick Sort completion within 48 hours."
        guardrail: "Day 0 drop-off before the first account connects, and the Day 2 return rate."
      - name: "Test 3: Transfers first"
        hypothesis: "Sorting transfers and card payments before merchants removes the biggest cause of wrong totals, so fewer categories need fixing later."
        control: "Biggest merchant groups first."
        variant: "Transfers and card payments first, with a one-line reason for each."
        primary: "Share of current-month transactions still uncategorized or edited again by Day 7."
        guardrail: "Time to finish Quick Sort, and the undo rate."
      - name: "Test 4: Give the partner a job"
        hypothesis: "A small task, like reviewing 3 transactions the AI wasn’t sure about, will get more partners active than a plain invite."
        control: "An invite from settings."
        variant: "“Ask your partner” on unsure Quick Sort cards, with an invite that opens on those cards."
        primary: "Partner active in week 2, per household that sent an invite."
        guardrail: "Invite-related support tickets, privacy complaints, and the inviter’s own Quick Sort completion."
